My Master's Thesis: _**"Are Cycles Cyclical? A Macroeconometric Enquiry into Endogenous Financial Fluctuations"**_, done at the *Paris School of Economics* (PSE) & the *Ecole Normale Supérieure* (ENS).

Supervised by Prof. Catherine Doz & Tobias Broer. Referee: Giovanni Ricco (CREST). _Summa cum laude._

This thesis investigates whether postwar U.S. macro-financial dynamics are consistent with endogenous cyclical fluctuations. Three complementary approaches are employed. First, recurrence, spectral, and wavelet analyses uncover stable medium-run periodicities around 9-10 years in financial stress and leverage, extending toward 12-15 years for stock-type variables, and cohering with real activity. Second, nonlinear polynomial and smooth-transition autoregressions reveal bounded local instability and limit-cycle-like dynamics rather than damped oscillations, with regime-dependent real-financial interactions. Third, Monte Carlo experiments show that while linear VARs approximate cycle length and detect qualitative feedbacks, nonlinear models are required to recover interaction magnitudes and diagnose instability. Together, the evidence suggests that U.S. macro-financial fluctuations cannot be reduced to linear shock persistence but are consistent with stochastic endogenous cycles.

**Keywords:** _Endogenous fluctuations, Financial cycle, Spectral & wavelet analysis, Limit cycles, Macroeconometrics, Macrofinance._
